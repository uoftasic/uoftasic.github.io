Students touch different aspects of IC design throughout the Electrical and Computer
Engineering curriculum, but are seldom given the chance to apply those skills to
design and fabricate a chip of their own. That is the gap we exist to close.

We partner with [Tiny Tapeout](https://tinytapeout.com/) and the Free and Open Source
Silicon [community](https://fossi-foundation.org/) to make that possible, using
open-source tooling for digital, mixed-signal and analog design. The team is open to
any interested student — no prior tapeout experience required.

## What an ASIC is

Application Specific Integrated Circuits are the cornerstone of modern electronics.
The process of fabricating one is known as *taping out*, for
[historical reasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tape-out#Naming).

## Our mission

1. Create practical IC design opportunities for students
2. Contribute back to the open-source silicon community
3. Build resources for anyone interested in IC design

## What we do

Our work runs across four areas, each throughout the academic year.

### Learning about IC design

**Internal education series** — asynchronous projects, documentation and guided
material introducing the open-source toolchain (LibreLane, OpenLane and friends),
to get interested engineers ready to design their own IC.

**Guest lectures** — talks from industry and academia that scale from classroom
knowledge to professional practice. Dates are on our
[Luma calendar]({{ site.organisation.events }}).

### Designing ICs

**Design Kompetition — proposals** — students propose and develop their own IC
projects on a rolling basis, submitting milestones, development structure and
technical documentation referencing existing designs.

**Design Kompetition — development** — selected projects move through design,
layout, optimisation, verification and validation, with tapeout typically targeted
around six months in.

**IC Hackathon** — a week-long design sprint where students onboard through
workshops and mentorship, then submit a design for judging. Our inaugural hackathon
in 2026 showed what undergraduates can do in seven days; we plan to run it during
reading week each spring.

### Growing the tools

**FOSSi Foundation contributions** — for students interested in the software
backbone of IC design, there is open-source CAD and EDA work used across the
community. Opportunities are announced as they open up.

### Building a community

**CanMUX** — a partnership with schools across Canada, including UWaterloo,
UCalgary and York, on the Canadian Microelectronics Undergraduate eXpo, an
undergraduate-focused IC design conference.

**Socials, graphics and webmaster** — growing how the world encounters
undergraduate IC design at U of T, and maintaining the technical infrastructure
behind it.

## The team

The team is led by **Vraj Prajapati** and **Saptarshi Talukdar**, with subteam
directors for Webmaster, Socials, CanMUX (U of T liaison), IC Hackathon
organisation, and Education. Director roles are filled through rolling recruitment.

We are building on two years of grassroots work. Our
[SimProc]({{ '/projects/simproc/' | relative_url }}) processor was taped out on the
Tiny Tapeout **TTSKY25b** shuttle in November 2025, and the 2026 IC Hackathon marked
a milestone for undergraduate IC design at U of T.
